# format() and lint() commit to the IR being sufficient

v1 adds two functions that turn the extraction library into a closed loop: `format(citation)` renders canonical Bluebook text from structured fields (the IR's inverse), and `lint(doc)` checks Bluebook practice (R4.1 id.-after-intervening-cite, short-form-before-full, signal ordering, missing pincites, nonstandard abbreviations). The architectural commitment worth recording: **lint is a pure consumer of the citation document — rules read only the IR, never the source text.** If a rule needs a fact the IR doesn't carry, the IR grows; the linter never re-parses. The rejected alternative (lint over raw text with its own analysis) would have created a second parser with its own drift. Same commitment powers the round-trip law — `extract(format(c))` recovers `c` — which becomes the rewrite's generative test net: format's semver contract is that law, not byte-stable output. Consequence: the IR's completeness is now load-bearing for three consumers (persistence, linting, formatting), which is deliberate — pressure on the IR to stay sufficient is pressure in the right place.
