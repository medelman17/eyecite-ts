# Confidence is categorical in v1; the numeric score is reserved, not shipped

v1 replaces `confidence: number` with `{ level: certain|high|medium|low, reasons: ReasonCode[] }` on citations and resolution edges, and reserves `score?: number` as a future minor addition permitted only alongside a committed calibration artifact with a published calibration error. The numeric score's track record is why: it was invented for the TS port (upstream Python eyecite has no confidence field), computed ad-hoc in ~40 places, and repeatedly miscalibrated by pipeline ordering (#147, #555, #556, #557, #613); a full remediation — the structured-Confidence overhaul with score/level/axes/reasons and ECE-gated calibration (PRs #486/#487, design spec and Phases 1–2 preserved on `feat/confidence-scoring-phase-1-2` and `beta/confidence-scoring`) — stalled precisely at the calibration phase because no labeled corpus existed (the ECE harness returned zero samples; the antecedent-annotator workbench in #829 that would produce one is also unfinished). So v1 ships the overhaul's honest parts (levels for stable cross-version semantics, reason codes for debuggability, one late scoring pass so levels can't be invalidated by later passes) and refuses to publish a number that implies a calibration nobody has performed. Considered and rejected: shipping the full struct with an "uncalibrated" caveat (consumers threshold on numbers regardless of caveats), and dropping confidence entirely (the resolver's graded abstention signals are load-bearing for consumers ranking resolutions — issue #832).
